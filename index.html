Pull-Adapted Belt
Pyramid · Aussie then pull-ups · 4-2 → 10-5 → 4-2
5 min▾
The Belt — Pull Version
Each step is a pair: Australian pull-ups first, then pull-ups. The Aussie number is double the pull-up number (e.g. "8-4" = 8 Aussie + 4 pull-ups). The only rest is the time it takes to move between the two bars.

Session 22 — Interlocked Pyramids
~1h30. One big pyramid across three exercises: push-ups and pull-ups climb then descend, while squats run the inverse. 11 sets, no rest inside a set. Totals: 180 push-ups, 205 squats, 79 pull-ups. Then a face/side plank pyramid.
~1h30 ★ Interlocked pyramid Inverse squats Endurance focus
🎙 Coach's message
"Pyramids for push-ups, squats and pull-ups. For push-ups and pull-ups it's a normal pyramid; for squats it's inverted. 11 sets, and for each you do push-ups, then squats, then pull-ups — no rest inside the set. Rest between sets, and take a lot of rest (2–3 min) around sets 5, 6, 7 — you'll need it. Too hard on push-ups? Use a higher bar. Too hard on pull-ups? Small band. Too easy? Dead-stop tempo to kill the rebound. It's all about muscular endurance."

Push-ups · Squats · Pull-ups — Interlocked Pyramid
11 sets · no rest inside a set
40 min▾
The rule
Each set = push-ups → squats → pull-ups, back-to-back with NO rest inside the set. Rest between sets. Push-ups and pull-ups climb to a peak at set 6 then descend; squats do the inverse (highest at the ends, lowest in the middle). Take extra rest (2–3 min) around sets 5, 6, 7.

Scaling
Push-ups too hard? Elevate your hands on a higher bar to reduce load. Pull-ups too hard? Use a small resistance band. Too easy? Use a dead-stop tempo — pause fully between every rep to kill the muscular rebound.
